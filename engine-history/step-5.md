### 5. The world
Designed on 31 October (the user: "Continue and finish the engine"). Today the core stops at a camera 18 km from its target (zoom 12.9), and beyond that today's map says "Zoom in to see the map". This step draws the world from there out to the whole planet (zoom 1, about 68,000 km), and lays it under every country's tiles until they come. Nothing is bundled (the user, 29 October: "either the netherlands or no map bundled with the app"): the world is fetched and kept.
- **Levels:**
  - the simplified level from the keep codes (done for the countries' frame cells on 30 October: one pack per country, step 7, L13 and L12);
  - blocks of merged footprints from about 5 km out (measured and left out there: they would change what the renders draw, step 7);
  - **the world's levels** go on from the countries' by doubling depth. Each starts where its tiles are about 910 dp wide, as L12's are at 9.4 km, and is simplified at the renders' 0.42 css px there, as L13 is at 1.5 m at 4.7 km:
    - L11 from 18.8 km at 6 m, L10 from 37.6 km at 12 m, L9 from 75 km at 24 m;
    - L8 from 150 km at 48 m, L7 from 300 km at 96 m, L6 from 600 km at 190 m;
    - L5 to L0 are the globe, from 1,200 km at 385 m to 38,000 km at 12 km.
    - World tiles carry no keep codes; their geometry is cut at their 4 × 4 cells.
  - **the world's cover** walks a quadtree from L0. A tile in view (inside the frustum, and in front of the horizon once the ground bends) is split while its nearest point is nearer than its level's start, down to L11.
    - The countries' cover is as today: windows to 4.7 km, L13 to 9.4 km, L12 to its reach (2·D, within 12 to 30 km). The world draws beyond that reach, and its cells under a resident country tile are skipped.
    - Up to a camera of 18 km the far plane is today's; beyond, it moves out as 30 km·(D/18 km)², up to the horizon.
  - **a tile's ancestor is drawn until the tile arrives,** at every level: a missing window is covered by its cell's L13, then L12, then the world's L11 and coarser, down to the globe, each ancestor drawing only its cells over the missing tile. The globe's L0 to L3 (0.92 MB, step 1's entry) stay resident, so once the globe is on the phone a zoom out never shows paper.
  - **fades:** a tile fades in over the cover's 0.18 s from the first frame it can be drawn, its ancestor under it until the fade ends. Nothing waits for a fade or a tile: each frame draws what is resident. The ground fades by alpha; what stands up by a dithered sample mask, in a pipeline variant used only while a tile fades, so depth stays right.
  - **the triangle governor** is the cover's second budget beside the heaps: the plan adds up its tiles' triangles (for a tile not built yet, its chunk's bytes times its level's measured triangles per byte).
    - The budget: 16 M triangles a frame planned, a third above the most any reference or VK bench path plans (step 4's entry: the 600k first written here was a twentieth of what the levels plan from 5 to 12 km), two thirds of it on phones with heaps under 128 MB until VK bench measures them.
    - Over budget, a bias moves every level's start and the countries' reach nearer, by at most 0.1 level a frame and 1.5 in all; under 80 % of the budget it comes back by 0.05 a frame. The countries' reach moves only where the world draws beyond it (elsewhere the paper would show), and a still camera's bias never comes back to one its plan was over at.
    - At bias 0 the cover is today's. Every reference case and VK bench path stays at bias 0 on the user's phone; where one does not, the budget is raised instead (no quality loss).
  - Held by: the walk (each level at its depth, no gap in the view, nothing behind the horizon); no frame held for a fade or a tile across a zoom from 1 to 100 km; the governor's bias in every reference.
- **The world's mid-zoom base (L6 to L11):**
  - **Data:**
    - land cover by the look's `LAND` kinds: same-kind polygons within 1 px merged, then any under 0.3 px² at the level's start dropped (the renders' far rule);
    - the land under everything and the sea outside it, from osmdata's land polygons, which fill each cell exactly (step 1's entry);
    - water: lakes, reservoirs and riverbanks, and rivers as lines down to L8;
    - roads by class: L11 to tertiary, L10 secondary, L9 primary, L8 trunk, L7 trunk and motorway, L6 motorway;
    - main railways to L9, runways at L11 and L10, built-up areas;
    - places with rank, population and names;
    - no buildings.
  - **Build:** Planetiler (Apache 2.0) runs Atlas's own profile, a Kotlin class in the packer that reads stage P's tag tables as data, so a field is the same kind on both sides of a country's edge. Planetiler's MBTiles is only a scratch file inside the job (its 4,096 units a tile are 0.22 px at a level's start): the packer cuts each tile again at its box and cells and writes format 3 (its layers, the 1e-7° grid with every vertex but a cut's on a lattice of at most 0.11 px at a level's start, the reach section; step 1's entry), which the core's decoder checks as it checks every pack. No OpenMapTiles schema or tiles.
  - **Packs:** a fine pack per L6 tile holds its L9 to L11 tiles, a coarse pack per L3 tile its L6 to L8 (at most 1,344 tiles each, a head of about 80 KB). About 1,500 fine and 25 coarse packs hold land; open sea has no tile. They lie end to end in parts under 1.9 GB (`world-mid.000`, …), and the world's index (`world.idx`, about 100 KB) gives each pack's block, levels, part, offset, length and SHA-256. The store's `world_mid` kind holds them (now L6 to L11) and `world_base` the globe (now fetched).
  - **Sizes,** estimated from Protomaps' planet scaled to these levels, good to a factor of two (the first Europe run measures them): L6 0.1 GB, L7 0.25, L8 0.6, L9 1.2, L10 2.5, L11 5, about 10 GB in all. A view at 30 km needs about 30 tiles, 0.2 to 1 MB the first time. The Netherlands' four L6 blocks measure 82 MB, about twice this scaling's 40 (step 1's entry).
  - **Fetch and keep:** `world.json` (3 KB, release `world-latest`) at most once a day; the index and the globe at the first start and each new version; a pack's head when a view first enters its block; then its tiles' chunks by ranges, neighbours in Hilbert order merged into one request, 8 requests a step, as the levels' reads are. Heads and chunks stay in the world's own LRU of 256 MB beside the windows' folder (a setting raises it), so a place seen once works offline. A new version drops the old one.
  - **Drawing:** the world pass has its own records and its own heap buffer, independent of the levels' records: a sixth of the geometry heaps' budget at first (42.7 MB at 256 MB, 21.3 MB at 128 MB, 10.7 MB at 64 MB), doubled where a view's tiles need it up to a third (85.3, 42.7 and 21.3 MB), each doubling within the device's budget (step 11). A tile no piece of the buffer holds whole lies in parts, each in the largest piece free (16 at most), and is drawn as one piece: its draws find each triangle's, segment's and light's part in a table of its parts (14 November, step 11), so its pixels are those of the tile whole. One draw per layer per tile, about 200 a frame at most, coarse to fine in painter's order (ground, water, lines, lights) with no depth, before the countries' tiles, which draw over it. Roads take the renders' terrain line rule, `max(0.5, w·focal/max(d, 600))` px, at most 4 dashed or 7 (relief.py's), a line under 1 px drawn 1 px wide at its coverage.
  - **The bend:**
    - Up to a camera of 18 km nothing moves, bit for bit.
    - Beyond, every vertex of every tile drops by w₁·|Δ|²/2R in `eye_relative` (Δ its offset from the target, R 6,371 km), w₁ easing in log D from 0 at 18 km to 1 at 150 km.
    - The world pass also blends each vertex by w₂, from 0 at 150 km to 1 at 600 km, to its place on the sphere tangent to the ground at the target, worked out per vertex from its latitude and longitude less the target's, in float with sines and cosines of the shader's own (`shared/sphere.slang`, within 2.5·10⁻⁷ of binary64: the GPU's sin and cos are good to 2⁻¹¹ only). Country tiles end by about 60 km, so none is bent differently from the world beside it.
    - Beyond 600 km the world is the sphere: cells behind the horizon culled, back faces too, lines and lights beyond it dropped per vertex.
    - Stage T cuts each world triangle and segment whose chord would sag more than half a pixel at its level's start (12 km at L11, twice as long two levels coarser), and each tile drawn finer than L3 has its L3 ancestor drawn under it (the floor), so the sub-pixel cracks where two levels meet on the bent ground show the ground. A tile's longitudes are taken within 180° of the target's, so the antimeridian is no seam.
  - **Hand-overs:** the countries' cover and the world's meet at tile edges, never inside a tile. The core draws every camera (step 3: since 5 November today's map has left the phone), the world's beyond 18 km and wherever no window is on the phone; the render packs' places are windows built from them (the windows' local lane). Until then `Transition.pathOf` gave the core every camera beyond 18 km once the globe was on the phone, and every place where today's map would say "No map data here", and today's map kept the places its render packs covered where the core had no window. Where no country's levels are, the world lies under the cover from every distance, and the core takes every camera beyond 9 km where the world holds the place's fine pack (step 3, 11 November). Windows built on the phone, and their levels, draw as today, with the world under them until they come.
  - Held by: the profile against stage P on the Netherlands' extract, every feature both write of the same kind, class and name (`:packer`); every world tile decoding in the host build, its ground covering its box (areas within 1e-9, no ring across a cell's edge); the world pass's twin and its cases (Checks 2) and the GPU against the twin on lavapipe (Checks 3); the seam over the Netherlands at 25 km pitched 60°, each kind's ground within 2 levels across it and a motorway's centreline within 0.5 px.
- **The globe (L0 to L5):**
  - **Data:** Natural Earth (public domain) for L0 to L3, 1:110m then 1:50m; OSM's coastline for L4 and L5 from osmdata's simplified land polygons, so the coast meets L6's without a step; Natural Earth's lakes, rivers, glaciers and ice shelves at each level's scale; places, countries and seas from OSM, so their names come in every script, ranked by Natural Earth's scalerank where their Wikidata ids match (until the planet is built, step 10, Natural Earth's populated places: step 1's entry).
  - **Build and fetch:** one pack of at most 1,365 tiles, about 15 MB (9.7 MB measured, step 1's entry), built by the same workflow, fetched at the first start as any world pack (its head, the view's chunks, about 1 MB, then the rest in the background) and kept in the app's files until a new version.
  - **First start,** before the globe comes: online, the plate (#D6CFC2) and "Getting the world map…"; offline, "The world map comes with the first connection". A country's tiles draw meanwhile, as today.
  - **Drawing:** the Globe board's Earth, with the values `CosmosArt.drawEarth` holds: the sea's radial ramp (#3A86C8, #1C5A9A at .6, #0B2A55) and the land's (#C9D8B4 to #E8EEF2), the coast, the graticule, the atmosphere's ring to 1.147 radii, the outline, the rim, the shine, and space behind. Both ramps are keyed to the surface's normal in the view, which on the board's disc is exactly its screen gradient.
  - **The true terminator,** per fragment: the sun's altitude at the surface, from the sub-solar point of the core's sun, gives the board's caps at 0°, −6°, −12° and −18°, at .50, .20, .15 and .12 of #030712, each edge a pixel soft.
  - **City lights,** one a place on the night side by the board's rule: night = clamp((−alt − 1)/10)·min(1, 3·cos θ); radius 2.6, 1.9 or 1.3 dp by rank; #FFE2A0, #FFD27A at .55 or #FFC45C at .25 by night, with a halo of 2.2 radii at .35. On the globe the places of scalerank ≤ 1 (about the board's 80), scalerank ≤ 4 at L3 to L5, the base's cities from L8, towns from L10.
  - **The poles:** no tiles beyond Mercator's 85.05°; the caps are drawn as sea in the north and Antarctica's land in the south.
  - Held by the globe check (the engine's frame at the board's camera and instant against `drawEarth` rasterised by Robolectric: a disc of radius 150 dp at (206, 420), tilt 20°, D about 54,000 km; MAD ≤ 1.5, the lights matched by place within 1 dp) and the poles' and open sea's references.
- **Names:**
  - **Data:** places in every tile (cities, towns, villages, suburbs at L11, countries); water (seas and oceans, lakes by area at their label point, rivers along their line from L11 to L9, flowing as the canal names do); each with `name` and up to 40 `name:*`.
  - **Shown:** `name` in its own script, as the renders draw Amsterdam's; inside the renders' regions only the boards' labels, as today.
  - **Drawing:** the core picks the view's names by rank, at most ten times a second while moving and once at rest, and asks the app for the sprites it lacks (id, text, kind: `MapCore.namesWanted`). The app draws them with `LabelPainter` and Android's text stack, which shapes every script with the system's fonts (the boards' faces where they hold the glyphs; a name the device has no glyphs for is skipped), and hands them back (`MapCore.names`). The labels pass draws each from the frame its sprite comes, fading in over 0.25 s. `labels/Placement` keeps names clear of each other by priority (the boards' labels, then water, then places by rank) and off the chrome, fades them out where they meet or leave, and never shows one behind the horizon.
  - **Sizes:** cities in the Main board's place style (17 dp #3A3F4B, halos 6 and 2 dp), towns 15 dp, villages 13; water in Instrument Serif italic #2F5A84; beyond 6,000 km the Globe board's Geist 700 12 dp; none beyond 20,000 km, where the board has none.

       - **What the design got wrong, and what changed:**
         - **The Netherlands' size:** 82 MB, where step 1 said about 40 MB (Maps' line, 50 to 100 MB over the country's tiles, holds). The rule is the renders' (0.42 px at a level's start, same kinds within 1 px merged, under 0.3 px² dropped), so it stays: at L11 the land cover is 78 % of the 16.3 M vertices and the inland water 16 % (at L10 grass is 39 % of the land cover's, forest 23 %, farmland 10 %). The planet's 10 GB is still Protomaps' scaling until Europe's run (step 5); the Netherlands, mapped closer than most, at twice its share says it may be more.
         - **The lattice** (`WorldGeometry.lattice`, the packer alone): every vertex but a cut's lies on a lattice of the grid, the largest power of two in each axis no wider than 1/32 of a tile's pixel (0.11 px at a level's start) at its row's poleward edge: at L11 128 units of longitude, and of latitude 128 to 53.4° and 64 from there to 72.6°.
